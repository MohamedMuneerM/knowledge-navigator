# Chemistry: curation audit

## 1. Verdict

**Before:** the discipline was an empty stub, with 0 chapters and no description.

**Now:** the file has 101 chapters and 1,081 topics in 20 categories. They start with a level-1 on-ramp (Introductory Chemistry, Lab Safety), cover the five ACS foundation areas and all the usual in-depth and applied areas, and end with research frontiers. Every chapter has a level, priority, summary and minimal prerequisites. The validator reports 0 errors and 0 warnings. The chapter count is deliberately higher than a typical chemistry degree. Chemistry has a large graduate layer (for example organometallic, bioinorganic and magnetic resonance), and the discipline also includes a Chemical Engineering block and the chemistry behind the rocket, Iron Man and cosmos roadmaps (combustion, propellants, energetic materials, batteries, fuel cells, high-performance materials, astrochemistry).

## 4. Grouping changes

This is a new discipline, so nothing was moved. The 20 categories run from foundational to applied: Foundations → General Chemistry → Laboratory & Professional Skills → Organic → Inorganic → Physical → Analytical & Instrumentation → Computational & Theoretical → Biochemistry & Chemical Biology → Medicinal & Pharmaceutical → Polymer, Materials & Solid-State → Surfaces, Catalysis & Supramolecular → Electrochemistry & Energy → Combustion, Propellants & Energetic Materials → Environmental & Green → Earth & Space → Nuclear & Radiochemistry → Chemical Engineering Fundamentals → Industrial & Applied → Frontiers.

Three placements were judgement calls:

- Symmetry & Group Theory sits under Inorganic, where ACS courses teach it, even though Physical spectroscopy depends on it.
- Solid-State Chemistry sits under Materials, not Inorganic.
- Electrochemistry heads the Energy category rather than sitting under Physical.

## 5. What was added

All 101 chapters are new. The most notable ones serve the goal roadmaps:

- For rockets: ch-combustion-chemistry, ch-propellant-chemistry (Isp thermochemistry, CEA-style equilibrium, LOX/RP-1, LOX/LH2 and methalox, hypergolics, composite solids, green and electric-propulsion propellants), ch-energetic-materials (conceptual: oxygen balance, CJ/ZND detonation theory, sensitivity, regulation), ch-fuels-petroleum-chemistry (RP-1, jet fuels, e-fuels), and radioisotope power in ch-applied-radiochemistry.
- For the Iron Man suit: ch-battery-chemistry (15 topics), ch-fuel-cells-hydrogen, ch-high-performance-materials (carbon fibre, aramids, polyimides, UHTCs, ablatives, armour, space-environment durability), ch-corrosion-science and ch-electronic-optical-materials.
- For the cosmos: ch-astrochemistry, ch-geochemistry-cosmochemistry, and planetary-atmosphere topics in ch-atmospheric-chemistry.

## 6. Learning-order notes

The main spine runs Introductory Chemistry → Atomic Structure → Bonding → States of Matter → Thermochemistry → Equilibrium → Acids/Bases and Redox → (Organic I → Stereochemistry → Organic Reactions I → Organic II) and (Descriptive Inorganic → Symmetry → Coordination) and (Chemical Thermodynamics + Quantum Chemistry → Kinetics, Spectroscopy, Statistical Thermodynamics) → specialisations.

Two branches serve the goal roadmaps:

- Rockets: Chemical Kinetics → Combustion → Propellants / Energetic Materials.
- Suit: Electrochemistry + Solid-State → Batteries / Fuel Cells, and Solid-State + Polymers → Materials Chemistry → High-Performance Materials.

The prerequisites taken from other disciplines are ma-calculus, ma-linear-algebra, ma-ordinary-differential-equations-odes, ma-partial-differential-equations-pdes, ma-descriptive-statistics, ma-multivariate-statistics, ai-core-ml-concepts and ph-quantum-information-science.

## 7. Recommended moves / open questions

- Chemical Engineering is covered here only as an applied introduction (5 chapters). If a full ChemE discipline is ever created, these chapters should move there or be linked to it.
- Physics covers the same subject from its side in ph-chemical-physics-physical-chemistry, which is linked via `related` from thermodynamics, quantum chemistry, kinetics, spectroscopy and reaction dynamics.
